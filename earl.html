From Earl’s mailbag

A few he’s already answered.

Why are some lines on wood poles and some on giant steel towers?

Different jobs. The steel towers carry transmission, the interstate of the grid, roughly 69,000 volts up to 765,000. The wood poles along your road carry distribution into town, usually about 4,000 to 35,000 volts. The can on top steps it down one last time for your house.

PLAUSIBLE · typical classes; vary by utility

Why do my lights blink two or three times in a storm, then come back?

That’s a recloser. It’s a switch on the line that opens when something touches a wire, waits a moment, and tries again. Lots of faults clear themselves. If it stays out, the fault didn’t, and a crew rolls. See a line down? Stay well back and call your co-op.

PLAUSIBLE · standard utility practice

Why did it take months to get power hooked up to my new shop?

Partly the transformer. The National Renewable Energy Laboratory found utilities waiting up to two years for distribution transformers, about four times the old normal, with prices up four to nine times in three years. Steel, copper, and crews are all short.

VERIFIED · NREL, 2024
