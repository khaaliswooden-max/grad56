Straight Talk · #7 · What people say

“There's nothing we can do; they'll build anyway.”

Our verdict

Wrong.

States, co-ops, and counties are setting terms, and some are saying no. Zoning, tariffs, benefit agreements, and co-op elections are all levers.

What the evidence shows

  • VERIFIEDAs of May 2026, 23 states had approved at least one large-load tariff.

    Source: Columbia Law School, Sabin Center for Climate Change Law (June 2, 2026), citing the Edison Electric Institute

  • VERIFIEDIn August 2026, Arizona regulators unanimously approved a process for six electric co-ops that makes large-load customers cover all the costs of serving them, including new transmission, so existing members don't subsidize them.

    Source: Arizona Corporation Commission (Aug. 13, 2026)

  • VERIFIEDIn Frederick County, Maryland, a developer offered $110 million in community benefits in exchange for an eight-year freeze on zoning changes that could affect its campus. After public comment, the county executive rejected the agreement on Sept. 14, 2026, and extended a moratorium on new data center applications to July 1, 2027.

    Source: WYPR (Sept. 15, 2026); Maryland Association of Counties (Sept. 2, 2026)
Where the critics are right

Deals are often negotiated behind closed doors (Straight Talk #10), and having levers doesn't mean they're easy to pull. PLAUSIBLE Frederick County said no only after residents spoke up.

What to ask for, in writing

  • Learn which levers your county holds: zoning, permits, abatements, benefit agreements.
  • Learn your co-op's: board elections, large-load tariffs, member meetings.

Correction. Corrected Nov. 26, 2026. This entry earlier said Frederick County “negotiated more than $110 million in developer-funded benefits.” The agreement was announced Sept. 1, 2026, but the county executive rejected it on Sept. 14.
