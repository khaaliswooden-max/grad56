Straight Talk · #12 · What people say

“My farm data is mine automatically.”

Our verdict

It depends on what you signed.

Industry principles say farmers own their data, but principles aren't law. The agreement you sign decides who can collect, use, share, and keep it.

What the evidence shows

  • VERIFIEDFarm groups and technology companies agreed on principles in 2015 that say farmers own the information generated on their operations. Ohio State University Extension describes these as guiding principles, and advises that ownership and control are set by explicit contracts, leases, and agreements. Ownership can be unclear when a landowner, a tenant, and a custom applicator share a field.

    Source: Ohio State University Extension, Fulton, Port & Colley (Feb. 9, 2018)
Where the critics are right

Farmers' distrust of how their data is collected and managed is real enough that federal auditors list data sharing and ownership as one of three main barriers to precision agriculture, with high costs and a lack of standards. VERIFIED (GAO-24-105962, Jan. 31, 2024)

What to ask for, in writing

  • Who can see the data, and why.
  • Whether it can be sold or shared.
  • Whether you can take it to another company.
  • What gets deleted when you leave.
