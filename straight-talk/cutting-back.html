Straight Talk · #11 · What people say

“Data centers can never cut back.”

Our verdict

Overstated.

Some can, some of the time, and the contract has to say how much.

What the evidence shows

  • VERIFIEDOne operator has put 1 gigawatt of data center demand response into long-term contracts with five utilities: Indiana Michigan Power, the Tennessee Valley Authority, Entergy Arkansas, Minnesota Power, and DTE Energy. That is the company's own announcement.

    Source: Google (Mar. 19, 2026)

  • VERIFIEDTexas law now requires new customers that connect at transmission voltage after 2025 to install equipment that lets their load be cut off during an emergency, and sets up a paid program for customers of 75 megawatts or more to cut back on notice.

    Source: Texas Senate Research Center, bill analysis of S.B. 6 (Aug. 5, 2025)
Where the critics are right

The same operator says there are limits to how flexible a data center can be, that the capability is available only at certain locations, and that it shifts only a portion of its workloads (Google, above). VERIFIED And a hall can meet a promise to cut its grid use by running diesel generators instead of using less. PLAUSIBLE

What to ask for, in writing

  • Megawatts: how much load the customer will drop.
  • Hours per year: how often it can be called.
  • The method: pausing work, batteries, or generators.
  • Who meters it, and who can read the meter.
  • The penalty for breaking the promise.
