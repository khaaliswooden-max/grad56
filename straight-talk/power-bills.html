Straight Talk · #1 · What people say

“Data centers are why my power bill went up.”

Our verdict

Overstated, with a real local risk.

Studies of 2015 to 2025 find small effects on home bills, sometimes lower prices. The risk is real where power is already tight.

What the evidence shows

  • VERIFIEDA causal study led by EPRI found that where data center capacity doubled, residential electricity prices fell about 4%, over 2015 to 2024.

    Source: EPRI, “Have Data Centers Raised Your Electric Bill?” (Watten, Bistline, and Blanford, 2026)

  • VERIFIEDA Wharton and Harvard Business School working paper estimates that data centers built from 2020 to 2025 explain about 4% of the rise in home electricity prices over that period, and about 15% of the rise in industrial prices. The paper had not been peer reviewed when it was reported.

    Source: Rana, Kaps, and Netessine, “The Cost of Intelligence” (Sept. 30, 2026), as summarized by FourWeekMBA from the paper's abstract

  • VERIFIEDWhere the grid was already congested, the story differs. A study for the Edison Electric Institute found PJM, the grid operator for much of the Mid-Atlantic, to be the one area where data centers have raised rates.

    Source: E&E News, Plautz and Marshall (Mar. 19, 2026)

  • PLAUSIBLEWhether your bill rises depends on your utility's terms. A big new customer that doesn't pay for its own substation or sign a long contract shifts risk onto everyone else on the system.

Where the critics are right

In PJM, where the grid was already congested, rates have gone up because of data centers (E&E News, above). And without a large-load tariff, members can end up paying for a substation built for one customer. PLAUSIBLE That worry is fair.

What to ask for, in writing

  • A large-load tariff: a separate rate class for big new customers.
  • A minimum bill, so they pay for the capacity they reserve.
  • A long contract and an exit fee, so members don't inherit the substation if they leave.
  • Collateral, and upgrades paid for up front.
