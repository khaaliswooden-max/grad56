Straight Talk · #2 · What people say

“A data center will drain our water.”

Our verdict

It depends on the cooling.

Evaporative cooling can use millions of gallons a day; closed-loop designs use about what a neighborhood does. Ask which kind before you argue.

What the evidence shows

  • VERIFIEDLarge data centers that cool by evaporating water can use up to 5 million gallons a day, about what a town of 10,000 to 50,000 people uses.

    Source: Environmental and Energy Study Institute, Yañez-Barnuevo (June 25, 2025)

  • VERIFIEDA closed-loop campus planned in Port Washington, Wisconsin, expects peak daily use of less than 22,000 gallons, which the developer compared to about 65 homes. Its closed-loop cooling would draw on the city water supply only in emergencies.

    Source: TMJ4 News (Sept. 19, 2025), quoting Vantage Data Centers and the city's mayor

  • VERIFIEDAbout three-quarters of the U.S. data center water footprint is indirect, at the power plants that supply the electricity, not at the building.

    Source: Siddik, Shehabi, and Marston (2021), Environmental Research Letters

  • PLAUSIBLESo a closed-loop design moves some of the water question to the power plant: dry cooling saves water on site by using more electricity, and electricity takes water upstream.

Where the critics are right

Evaporative designs really can strain a local supply, especially in a drought, and many operators haven't measured or reported their water use. Asking hard questions here is reasonable.

What to ask for, in writing

  • The cooling type, in writing.
  • Peak and average daily gallons, and where the water comes from.
  • A drought clause that cuts use during water emergencies.
  • Public, metered reporting of water use.
