Power, chips, and data centers, explained from the ground up for rural America.

Data centers are heading for rural counties. Some of the fears about them are overstated, and some are exactly right. Grid & Grain tells the difference out loud, with sources, then shows your community what to ask for, whether it decides yes or no. For county leaders, co-op members, farmers, and rural hospital teams.

Straight Talk library

What people say, what the evidence shows, and what to ask for.

We grade the claim, never the person. Figures are dated to their sources and rechecked before reuse. Last reviewed November 2026.

01 / Rates

“Data centers are why my power bill went up.”

Overstated, with a real local risk

An EPRI-led causal study (2015–2024) found a doubling of data center capacity lowered residential prices about 4% (EPRI). A Wharton and Harvard Business School paper attributes about 4% of the 2020–2025 residential price rise to data centers, and 15% of the industrial rise. Effects concentrate in tight markets such as PJM (E&E News).

Ask for: a large-load tariff with a separate rate class, minimum bills, long contracts, exit fees, and prepaid upgrades.

02 / Water

“A data center will drain our water.”

It depends on the cooling

Evaporative designs can use up to 5 million gallons a day. A closed-loop campus in Port Washington, Wisconsin, plans a peak near 22,000 gallons a day, about 65 homes (Vantage). About three-quarters of the U.S. data center water footprint sits upstream at power plants (Siddik et al., 2021).

Ask for: cooling type, peak and average gallons, water source, and a drought curtailment clause.

VERIFIED
03 / Jobs

“It’ll bring hundreds of jobs.”

Wrong for permanent jobs

Rural facilities often employ fewer than 100 permanent workers, and specialized services come from outside the county (Georgia Tech via Futurity). Construction jobs are real but temporary.

Ask for: local-hire targets, apprenticeships, and a community college partnership, in writing.

VERIFIED
04 / Taxes

“It will pay for our schools.”

Partly right

It can: Loudoun County, Virginia, reports the industry generates nearly half its property tax revenue. Abatements can erase much of it: El Paso granted an 80% property tax abatement over 35 years (Brookings).

Ask for: the abatement terms and the net revenue to each taxing district, school board first.

VERIFIED
05 / Noise

“They’re 96 decibels, 24 hours a day.”

Overstated as a blanket claim

Readings next to machines don’t equal levels at a home (Linn Valley FAQ). But noise complaints are real: neighbors in Vineland, New Jersey, have sued over round-the-clock noise. Sanford, North Carolina, caps data centers at 65 dBA at the property line (NC Newsline).

Ask for: a dBA limit at the property line, low-frequency measurement, setbacks, generator test hours, and third-party monitoring.

VERIFIED
06 / Scale

“AI already uses most of America’s electricity.”

Wrong nationally

U.S. data centers used 4.4% of U.S. electricity in 2023, with a 2028 range of 6.7% to 12% (Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, 2024). Nearly half of U.S. capacity sits in five regional clusters (International Energy Agency, 2025), so the local share is what matters.

Ask for: the share of your utility’s or co-op’s peak demand a project would be.

VERIFIED
07 / Leverage

“There’s nothing we can do. They’ll build anyway.”

Wrong

As of May 2026, 23 states had approved at least one large-load tariff (Columbia Sabin Center). Frederick County, Maryland, negotiated more than $110 million in developer-funded benefits (MACo). Arizona’s co-ops won a framework that keeps large-load costs off existing members (ACC).

Learn the levers: zoning, utility tariffs, benefit agreements, and co-op board elections.

VERIFIED
08 / Health

“AI does nothing for rural people.”

Wrong, but adoption is thin

At Central Montana Medical Center, a 25-bed Critical Access Hospital, ambient AI scribing cut after-hours charting so much that some physicians said they’d consider leaving without it. Only about 8% of critical access and rural community hospitals use AI-driven predictive analytics (Becker’s).

Ask for: any local data center deal to include broadband and AI-readiness support for the hospital and schools.

VERIFIED
09 / Forecasts

“Data center power use will double forever.”

Plan in ranges

From 2010 to 2018, global data center electricity rose only about 6% while computing grew about 6.5 times (Masanet et al., 2020, Science). AI broke that balance after 2017, and forecasts now span nearly a factor of two.

Ask for: low, base, and high scenarios in any utility plan, and who pays if demand lands low.

VERIFIED
10 / Transparency

“These deals are done in secret.”

Often right

Local officials have been asked to sign nondisclosure agreements, and secrecy is a leading driver of opposition (Iowa Public Radio).

Ask for: a transparency clause: a named end user, a public hearing before any vote, and no NDAs for elected officials.

VERIFIED
