Straight Talk: what people say, graded

All 12 entries →

We grade the claim, never the person. Each entry’s page links every source with its date. Figures last reviewed November 2026.

We don’t have a graded entry on that yet.

Try one plain word, like “water” or “taxes.” Or send us the claim: we add one entry a month, and we won’t guess at an answer before it’s sourced. All twelve entries are below.

  • #01 · Rates

    “Data centers are why my power bill went up.”

    Overstated, with a real local riskVERIFIEDPLAUSIBLE for the future

    An EPRI-led study of 2015–2024 found that doubling data center capacity lowered residential prices about 4%. The risk concentrates where supply is tight, such as the PJM region. Source: EPRI; E&E News.

    Ask for: a large-load tariff: separate rate class, minimum bills, long contracts, exit fees, prepaid upgrades.

  • #02 · Water

    “A data center will drain our water.”

    It depends on the coolingVERIFIED

    Evaporative designs can use up to 5 million gallons a day. A closed-loop campus in Port Washington, Wisconsin, plans a peak near 22,000 gallons a day, about 65 homes. Source: Vantage Data Centers.

    Ask for: a water disclosure: cooling type, peak and average gallons, source, and a drought curtailment clause.

  • #03 · Jobs

    “It’ll bring hundreds of jobs.”

    Wrong for permanent jobsVERIFIED

    Rural facilities often employ fewer than 100 permanent workers, and specialized services come from outside the county. Construction jobs are real but temporary. Source: Georgia Tech, via Futurity.

    Ask for: local-hire targets, apprenticeships, and a community college partnership, in writing.

  • #04 · Taxes

    “It will pay for our schools.”

    Partly rightVERIFIED

    Loudoun County, Virginia, reports the industry generates nearly half its property tax revenue. Abatements can erase much of it: El Paso granted 80% over 35 years. Source: Straight Arrow News; Brookings.

    Ask for: the abatement terms and the net revenue to each taxing district, school board first.

  • #05 · Noise

    “They’re 96 decibels, 24 hours a day.”

    Overstated; complaints are realVERIFIED

    Readings next to machines aren’t levels at a home. Neighbors in Vineland, New Jersey, have sued over round-the-clock noise. Sanford, North Carolina, caps data centers at 65 dBA at the property line. Source: Linn Valley FAQ; NC Newsline, July 15, 2026.

    Ask for: a dBA limit at the property line, low-frequency measurement, setbacks, generator test hours, and third-party monitoring.

  • #06 · Scale

    “AI already uses most of America’s electricity.”

    Wrong nationally; local share mattersVERIFIED

    U.S. data centers used 4.4% of U.S. electricity in 2023, with a 2028 range of 6.7% to 12%. Nearly half of U.S. capacity sits in five regional clusters. Source: Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, 2024; International Energy Agency, 2025.

    Ask for: the share of your utility’s or co-op’s peak the project would be.

  • #07 · Leverage

    “There’s nothing we can do; they’ll build anyway.”

    WrongVERIFIED

    As of May 2026, 23 states had approved at least one large-load tariff. Frederick County, Maryland, turned down a $110 million benefits offer on Sept. 14, 2026, because it would have frozen local zoning for eight years. Source: Columbia Sabin Center, June 2, 2026; WYPR, Sept. 15, 2026. Corrected Nov. 26, 2026.

    Ask for: a seat at the table before the vote. The levers: zoning, tariffs, benefit agreements, co-op board elections.

  • #08 · Health

    “AI does nothing for rural people.”

    Wrong, but adoption is thinVERIFIED

    At Central Montana Medical Center, a 25-bed critical access hospital, AI scribing cut after-hours charting. Becker’s puts AI-driven predictive analytics at about 8% of critical access and rural community hospitals, without naming its survey (PLAUSIBLE). Source: Becker’s Hospital Review.

    Ask for: broadband and AI-readiness funding for the hospital and schools, tied to any local deal.

  • #09 · Forecasts

    “Data center power use will double forever.”

    Plan in rangesVERIFIED

    From 2010 to 2018, global data center electricity rose about 6% while computing grew about 6.5 times. AI broke that balance after 2017, and forecasts now span nearly a factor of two. Source: Masanet et al., 2020; Shehabi et al., 2024.

    Ask for: low, base, and high scenarios in any utility plan.

  • #10 · Transparency

    “These deals are done in secret.”

    Often rightVERIFIED

    Local officials have been asked to sign nondisclosure agreements, and secrecy is a leading driver of opposition. Source: Iowa Public Radio, Sept. 2, 2026.

    Ask for: a transparency clause: named end user, public hearing before any vote, no NDAs for elected officials.

  • #11 · Flexibility

    “Data centers can never cut back.”

    OverstatedVERIFIED

    One operator has 1 gigawatt of demand response under contract with five utilities, and Texas now requires new high-voltage customers to be able to be cut off in an emergency. Source: Google, Mar. 2026; Texas S.B. 6 bill analysis, Aug. 2025.

    Ask for: megawatts, hours per year, the method, who meters it, and the penalty for breaking the promise.

  • #12 · Farm data

    “My farm data is mine automatically.”

    It depends on what you signedVERIFIED

    Industry principles say farmers own their data, but they are principles, not law; the signed agreement decides. Federal auditors list data distrust as a barrier to adoption. Source: Ohio State University Extension, 2018; GAO, Jan. 2024.

    Ask for: who sees the data, whether it can be sold, whether you can take it with you, and what is deleted when you leave.
