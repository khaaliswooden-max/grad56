Ask in writing

  1. Of the permanent jobs, how many will be on your payroll, at what wages, and how many do you expect to fill from this county?
  2. At your other sites, what share of operating staff live in the host county today?
  3. If the hiring targets are missed, what changes in the incentive?

Why these questions

  • Split the number. Rural facilities often employ fewer than 100 permanent workers, and many specialized services come from outside the county (Georgia Tech, as reported by Futurity, July 2026). VERIFIED Construction jobs are real and temporary.
  • Who paid for the study, and compared with what. Counties that attract data centers were already growing faster, so simple comparisons overstate the job effect (Bahar and Wright, Brookings, Aug. 2026). VERIFIED
  • Dollars per job. The same study found state incentives ranging from about 2% of construction investment in some counties to about 62% in others. VERIFIED The division on this sheet shows where your deal sits.
  • Define “local” and set targets. Without a definition, a traveling worker who rents a room counts. PLAUSIBLE Whether your county may require local hiring, and how, depends on state law; ask the county attorney first.
  • Training money is not a hiring commitment. A $110 million offer to Frederick County, Maryland, included $14.5 million for a workforce center and no local hiring requirement; the county executive rejected the agreement after public comment (WYPR, Sept. 15, 2026). VERIFIED
  • The operator needs this too. More than half of data center operators surveyed in 2026 had difficulty finding qualified candidates (Uptime Institute, July 2026). VERIFIED That is the county’s leverage for a funded training partnership.
