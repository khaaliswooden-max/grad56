Ask in writing

  1. Will the full agreement and exhibits be public at least 30 days before any vote?
  2. Has any county official signed a nondisclosure agreement on this project? Until when?
  3. Do a large-load tariff and a zoning ordinance already cover power costs, noise, and water?

Why these questions

  • The signer decides who can be held to the contract. A benefit agreement is a legally binding contract between a developer and a local government, community groups, or both (Sabin Center, May 2026). VERIFIED A company created for one project is one path; ask who stands behind it (Module 10, Rung 3).
  • Numbers can be enforced; adjectives can’t. Lancaster, Pennsylvania, caps water at 20,000 gallons a day per campus, but asks only for “good faith efforts” on local hiring (City of Lancaster; Sabin Center, May 2026). VERIFIED
  • The net is the honest number. El Paso granted an 80% property tax abatement over 35 years (Brookings Institution, Jan. 2026). VERIFIED
  • Security is rare. Bonds for cleanup or financial assurance appeared in 4 of 42 local data center actions reviewed (Federation of American Scientists, Sept. 2026). VERIFIED
  • Transfer terms matter because transfers often happen without the host government’s consent (Federation of American Scientists, Sept. 2026). VERIFIED
  • The 30-day question comes from Frederick County, Maryland, where residents had ten days to comment on a proposal worth more than $110 million before the county executive rejected it (WYPR, Sept. 2026). VERIFIED Thirty days is our suggestion, not a legal standard. SPECULATIVE
