Making the Deal: five questions

Answer from memory first. Four right is a pass, and you can retake it as often as you like. Sign in to save your score toward the Field Certificate.

  1. QUESTION 1What makes a community benefit agreement different from a promise in a press release?

    Why: A benefit agreement is a legally binding contract between a developer and a local government, community groups, or both (Sabin Center, May 2026). A press release can’t be collected.

  2. QUESTION 2Which of these terms could a county actually enforce?

    Why: A term can be enforced only if someone can measure whether it was met. A gallon cap can be read off a meter. “Good faith” and “where feasible” can’t.

  3. QUESTION 3What does a letter of credit do in a benefit agreement?

    Why: Security sets the money aside ahead of time. A letter of credit is a bank’s written promise to pay if the developer fails to, so the county isn’t chasing a company that has left.

  4. QUESTION 4A developer makes three promises, all owed by one company created for the project. Why is that weaker than it looks?

    Why: Copies are not independence. Like three mesh Wi-Fi points behind one modem, three promises on one path share a single point of failure. Independence means a bank holding the money, obligations recorded on the land, and a third party doing the measuring.

  5. QUESTION 5Frederick County, Maryland, was offered more than $110 million in benefits in September 2026. What happened?

    Why: The county would have agreed not to make zoning changes that hurt the project for eight years. After hundreds of public comments in a ten-day window, the county executive rejected the agreement on Sept. 14, 2026 (WYPR). The biggest number is one term out of many.

Answer all five questions first.
