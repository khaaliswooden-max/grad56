Why AI Eats Power: five questions

Answer from memory first. Four right is a pass, and you can retake it as often as you like. Sign in to save your score toward the Field Certificate.

  1. QUESTION 1Where does nearly all the electricity that goes into a computer end up?

    Why: Every switch flip moves charge, and that energy leaves as heat. A building full of computers is a building full of heaters, which is why cooling decides so much.

  2. QUESTION 2A chip’s power use rises with its clock speed and with its voltage in what way?

    Why: Power is proportional to clock speed times voltage squared (National Research Council, 2011). That’s why a 60% faster clock that needs about 12% more voltage roughly doubles the power.

  3. QUESTION 3Compared with adding two numbers, fetching a number from a computer’s main memory takes about how much energy?

    Why: Stanford engineers put it at 500 to 3,500 times, depending on the size of the number. Most of the energy goes to moving data, not doing math, and AI moves an enormous amount of data.

  4. QUESTION 4Each AI answer is getting cheaper in energy, yet total data center electricity keeps rising. Why are both true?

    Why: Efficiency per answer and total power are different numbers. U.S. data centers used about three times as much electricity in 2023 as in 2014 (Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory). When someone gives you one number, ask for the other.

  5. QUESTION 5A developer’s application lists a 300-megawatt “nameplate” load. What is the best first question?

    Why: Nameplate is the most a site could ever draw. One city utility reports its data centers peak at about 67% of nameplate, and sites take years to ramp up (Energy and Environmental Economics, Dec. 2025).

Answer all five questions first.
