Sun, Storage, and Flexible Load: five questions

Answer from memory first. Four right is a pass, and you can retake it as often as you like. Sign in to save your score toward the Field Certificate.

  1. QUESTION 1Why does a co-op ask members to use less on a hot afternoon?

    Why: Peak times are when a co-op buys wholesale power at its highest prices, and the peak sets the size of plants, lines, and substations. Shaving the peak lowers costs for every member.

  2. QUESTION 2What is “net load”?

    Why: Net load is the part other power plants have to cover. On a sunny day it sags at midday and climbs steeply toward sunset, which is the shape called the duck curve.

  3. QUESTION 3What does a grid battery do?

    Why: A battery doesn’t make energy. It fills when power is plentiful, empties when power is short, and loses a little along the way. Most grid batteries today discharge for a few hours.

  4. QUESTION 4A Duke University study estimated how much new load the existing grid could take if that load cut back 0.25% of the time. About how much?

    Why: 76 gigawatts at 0.25% of the time, and 126 gigawatts at 1%. It’s a system-wide estimate for the 22 largest grid areas, not a promise about any one substation.

  5. QUESTION 5What did that study leave out, by its own account?

    Why: The study didn’t examine transmission limits. Room on the whole system isn’t room at your substation, which is why a county still has to ask its own utility.

Answer all five questions first.
