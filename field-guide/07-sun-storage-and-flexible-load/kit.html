Ask in writing

  1. On our system’s peak day last year, what would this customer have been asked to do, and for how long?
  2. How many megawatts of the promised reduction come from using less power, from batteries, and from generators?
  3. Where will the curtailment record be published, and what happens after a missed event?

Why these questions

  • Hours and megawatts are the whole promise. A Duke University study estimated the existing grid could take 76 gigawatts of new load that cut back about 85 hours a year, system-wide, without looking at local lines (reported by Utility Dive, Feb. 2025). VERIFIED Your utility has to say what the number is at your substation.
  • Who can call for it separates a real obligation from a courtesy. Texas law requires new customers that connect at transmission voltage to install equipment that lets their load be cut during an emergency (Texas S.B. 6 bill analysis, Aug. 2025). VERIFIED
  • The method decides what the neighbors breathe. Cutting back by starting diesel generators lowers demand on the grid and raises exhaust on the hottest days (Module 7, Rung 5).
  • Generator hours already have a federal ceiling: emergency engines may run 100 hours a year outside emergencies, including up to 50 for demand response under set conditions (EPA guidance, reported by the American Public Power Association, May 2025). VERIFIED A local permit or agreement can ask for less. Whether it may is a question for your county attorney.
  • The meter and the consequence turn a press release into a term. One large operator says outright that flexibility has limits and exists only at some sites (Google, March 2026). VERIFIED A discount for flexibility with no penalty for failing is a discount. PLAUSIBLE
