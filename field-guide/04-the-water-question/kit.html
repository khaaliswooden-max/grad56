Why these questions

  • Cooling type decides most of the on-site water. Large data centers can use up to 5 million gallons a day (Environmental and Energy Study Institute, June 2025). VERIFIED A closed-loop campus planned in Port Washington, Wisconsin, says its peak will be under 22,000 gallons a day (TMJ4 News, Sept. 2025). VERIFIED as the company’s stated plan.
  • Peak-day gallons size the pipes and pumps. Data center peak days are estimated at 3 to 10 times their average, against 1.5 to 2.5 for ordinary customers (Han and colleagues, Mar. 2026). PLAUSIBLE
  • Share evaporated. Evaporated water doesn’t return to the river or the aquifer. In evaporative systems about 80% of the water taken in evaporates (Environmental and Energy Study Institute, June 2025). VERIFIED
  • The pipe. If new water infrastructure is built for one customer, the terms from the large-load tariff checklist apply: who pays, and what happens if the customer leaves.
  • Reporting. A 2021 review found fewer than a third of operators tracked their water use (Mytton, npj Clean Water, 2021). VERIFIED Virginia will require monthly reporting of water delivered to data centers from January 1, 2027 (National Conference of State Legislatures, Aug. 2026). VERIFIED
  • Upstream water. About three-quarters of the U.S. data center water footprint is at power plants (Siddik and colleagues, 2021). VERIFIED It may be in another county, but it belongs in the picture.
