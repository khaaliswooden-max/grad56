Ask before anyone buys hardware

  3. What do we pay now for the same services, in total, across every member?

Why these questions

  • Members first, because pooling is the whole idea. Users with thin, scattered demand end up in computing’s slow lane (Thompson & Spanuth, 2021). VERIFIED Farm co-ops solved the same problem for electricity: about nine in ten rural homes lacked service in the mid-1930s, and more than 90% of farms had it by 1953 (NRECA). VERIFIED
  • The four check boxes are the four advantages of computing close to home: fast response, less data to send, privacy, and riding through an outage (Satyanarayanan, 2017). VERIFIED A service that needs none of them is cheaper in the cloud.
  • Technicians lead the pooled list because managing scattered sites is the first obstacle the research names (Satyanarayanan, 2017). VERIFIED
  • Two sites because copies aren’t independence (Module 10, Rung 3).
  • Voting and exit follow the cooperative principles, including democratic member control (NRECA). VERIFIED Ohio law keeps its school technology centers honest by letting any district buy services elsewhere (Ohio Revised Code 3301.075). VERIFIED
  • The replacement reserve is our own suggestion. PLAUSIBLE
