The Rural Hospital Runs on Compute: five questions

Answer from memory first. Four right is a pass, and you can retake it as often as you like. Sign in to save your score toward the Field Certificate.

  1. QUESTION 1Which of these is a condition for being a Critical Access Hospital?

    Why: A Critical Access Hospital has 25 or fewer inpatient beds, is usually more than 35 miles from the next hospital, keeps average stays to 96 hours or less, and runs a 24/7 emergency room (Rural Health Information Hub).

  2. QUESTION 2Where does an AI scribe usually do its computing?

    Why: The sound leaves the exam room and travels to a data hall that may be several states away. That’s why the hospital’s connection and its contract matter as much as the software.

  3. QUESTION 3What does the rural health toolkit say about notes drafted by an AI scribe?

    Why: Reported problems include invented content, missed details, and words pinned on the wrong speaker. The toolkit calls human review a necessity, and that review takes back some of the time saved.

  4. QUESTION 4As of June 2025, about what share of rural Americans could get wired or fixed-wireless internet at 100/20 megabits?

    Why: 86.7%, against 96.9% for the whole country (FCC 2026 Section 706 Report). That counts where a provider says service is available, not what a building actually receives, so a hospital should run its own tests.

  5. QUESTION 5A scribe drafting a note during a visit is an example of what?

    Why: Training builds a model once in a giant data hall. Inference is using it, and every visit is a new job sent to a computer somewhere. Where that computer sits is the question this module asks.
