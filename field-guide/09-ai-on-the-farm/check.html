AI on the Farm: five questions

Answer from memory first. Four right is a pass, and you can retake it as often as you like. Sign in to save your score toward the Field Certificate.

  1. QUESTION 1What does “precision agriculture” mean?

    Why: A planter that changes its rate with the soil, a sprayer that sprays the weed and skips bare ground, a yield monitor that maps the harvest. Each is a computer making a decision about one piece of ground.

  2. QUESTION 2Why does a camera-guided sprayer make its spray decision on the machine instead of asking a distant data hall?

    Why: Distance is delay. A decision tied to a moving machine has a fraction of a second, so it’s made at the edge, on the machine. Slow decisions, like next year’s seed, can go to the cloud.

  3. QUESTION 3In a 2025 federal survey, about what share of U.S. farms used precision agriculture practices?

    Why: 22% in 2025, down from 27% in 2023 (USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service). Cost, brands that won’t work together, and distrust over data are the obstacles federal auditors list.

  4. QUESTION 4The broadband map shows coverage on your farm, but a tool needs signal in the far field. What’s the reading that counts?

    Why: Survey and map figures describe the house and what a provider says is available. A phone speed test at the house, the field edge, and the far fence is a plausible reading from the spot that matters.

  5. QUESTION 5What is latency?

    Why: Speed is how much data moves per second. Latency is how long one round trip takes. A tool that uploads maps overnight cares about speed; one that needs an answer while moving cares about latency.
