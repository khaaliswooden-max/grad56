The Wire to Your Porch: five questions

Answer from memory first. Four right is a pass, and you can retake it as often as you like. Sign in to save your score toward the Field Certificate.

  1. QUESTION 1Why does the grid raise voltage for long trips?

    Why: P = V × I, so the same power at higher voltage needs less current. Heat lost in a wire grows with current squared: one fifth the current means one twenty-fifth the loss.

  2. QUESTION 2About what share of U.S. electricity did data centers use in 2023?

    Why: About 4.4% in 2023 (Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory). The 12% figure is the top of the lab’s 2028 range, not today. Locally the share can be much higher.

  3. QUESTION 3Across U.S. balancing areas from 2016 to 2024, average demand was about what share of peak?

    Why: About 53% (Duke University). The grid is built for its worst hour, so much of it sits idle most of the time. That idle room is why flexible loads can connect sooner.

  4. QUESTION 4Who owns a rural electric co-op?

    Why: A co-op is member-owned, and members elect the board. That’s why members can ask that big new loads pay their own way.

  5. QUESTION 5What makes the grid different from a town’s water system?

    Why: A water tower holds days of supply. The grid’s wires hold almost none, so it’s a juggler catching every ball the moment it drops.
