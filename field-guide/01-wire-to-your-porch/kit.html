Why these questions

  • Who owns it decides who you can hold to account. Co-op members elect their board, one member, one vote (University of Wisconsin Center for Cooperatives). VERIFIED An investor-owned utility’s rates are set by the state utility commission in a public rate case (Module 1, Rung 4).
  • The election date and annual meeting are when members have the most say. Co-op annual meeting season starts in February.
  • Rate classes show whether big customers are billed separately from homes and farms. A separate class for large loads is the first line of a large-load tariff (Straight Talk #1).
  • A large-load tariff is how members avoid paying for someone else’s substation. As of May 2026, 23 states had approved at least one (Columbia Sabin Center, in Straight Talk #7). VERIFIED The large-load tariff checklist walks through each term.
  • The connection line tells you how much leverage your community has. A project that wants to connect sooner has reasons to accept terms (Module 1, Rung 5).
  • Share of peak is the number that matters locally. Data centers used about 4.4% of U.S. electricity in 2023 (Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory), but one project can be a large share of one utility’s system. VERIFIED
