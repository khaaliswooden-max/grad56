Who Pays for the Substation?: five questions

Answer from memory first. Four right is a pass, and you can retake it as often as you like. Sign in to save your score toward the Field Certificate.

  1. QUESTION 1A co-op builds a substation for one large customer, and that customer leaves early. With no large-load tariff in place, who is left paying for it?

    Why: The equipment and the loan stay behind, and the co-op recovers its costs from the members it still has. A large-load tariff exists to keep that from happening.

  2. QUESTION 2What does a minimum bill do?

    Why: The utility built for what the customer reserved. In Ohio the floor is as high as 85% of contracted capacity: reserve 100 megawatts, use 40, and the bill is still figured on 85.

  3. QUESTION 3As of May 2026, how many states had approved at least one large-load tariff?

    Why: 23 states, with 7 more pending (Edison Electric Institute, as cited by Columbia’s Sabin Center, June 2026). That’s why “there’s nothing we can do” is wrong.

  4. QUESTION 4Which of these is NOT one of the six tariff terms in this module?

    Why: The six are a separate rate class, a minimum bill, collateral, an exit fee, a long contract, and upgrades paid up front. A promise isn’t a term. Ask for it in writing.

  5. QUESTION 5Two major studies found different effects of data centers on home power prices. What best explains how both can be right?

    Why: More sales can lower the price per kilowatt-hour; new substations, lines, and tighter supply can raise it. The six terms are how a community makes sure the new customer covers the costs it causes.

Answer all five questions first.
