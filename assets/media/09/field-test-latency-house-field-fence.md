# Field Test 09: Latency at the house, the field edge, and the far fence

Module 9, *AI on the Farm*. Khaalis films; about 4 to 5 minutes finished. Horizontal 1920×1080 for the module page and YouTube; a vertical 1080×1920 cut of shots 4–8 for Shorts and Facebook. Outdoors, daylight, any season; harvest footage in September is a bonus, not a requirement.

## Safety and rules (read before filming)

- **Phone only.** No equipment is opened, climbed, or modified. Stay off towers, poles, bins, and anything with a ladder.
- **Never from a moving machine.** Every reading is taken standing still, on foot or beside a parked vehicle with the engine off. Say so on camera.
- **Permission.** Film on ground you own or have the owner's say-so to be on. If it's a neighbor's or a host farm's field, get permission in writing and don't show house numbers, mailbox names, or anything that identifies a family who hasn't agreed.
- Stay clear of working equipment, livestock, and anything overhead. If a power line crosses the field, don't stand under it for the shot and never raise anything toward it.
- **Phone-app readings are PLAUSIBLE, never VERIFIED.** The on-screen line "Phone-app reading: PLAUSIBLE" appears with every number. One phone, one carrier, one afternoon: never present it as a coverage map of the county or a rating of the carrier.
- Name the phone, the carrier, the app, and the day on screen and in the narration. Report readings exactly as measured, including a failed test.

## Readings to log (fill in on the day)

Run the test **three times at each stop**, a minute apart. Use the middle latency in the narration and show all three on the closing card.

Also log: date and time; phone make and model; carrier; app name; home internet provider and type; weather.

If a test won't start or fails, write "no result" and keep it. A dead spot is a finding.

The arithmetic for the closing card, using your far-fence latency:

- Feet traveled at 15 miles an hour = 22 feet per second × (latency in ms ÷ 1,000).
- Example only: at 120 ms, 22 × 0.120 = about 2.6 feet. Use your own number.

## Narration script

Read by Khaalis in his own voice, or rendered in Kokoro am_michael at speed 1.0 (then the video also carries "Narration: AI voice"). Brackets are filled in from the log; read numbers as words.

> This is a Grid and Grain Field Test. Phone only, and never from a moving machine.
>
> It's [day of week], [month day], near [town]. I've got a [phone make and model] on [carrier], and a free speed-test app called [app name]. It measures three things: download, upload, and latency. Latency is the delay for one round trip, in milliseconds, which are thousandths of a second.
>
> A phone app isn't a lab instrument, so every number you're about to see is graded plausible, not verified.
>
> First, at the house, on the home internet. [download] megabits down, [upload] up, and [latency] milliseconds.
>
> Same spot, Wi-Fi off, so now it's the cell network. [download] down, [upload] up, [latency] milliseconds, [bars] bars.
>
> Now the edge of the field, about [distance] from the house. Standing still. [download] down, [upload] up, [latency] milliseconds, [bars] bars.
>
> And the far fence, about [distance] out. [Result: download, upload, latency, bars. Or: The test won't run. No result. That's a finding too.]
>
> I ran each one three times. They're all on screen, as measured.
>
> Here's why latency matters. A sprayer at fifteen miles an hour covers twenty-two feet every second. If it had to wait [far-fence latency] milliseconds for an answer from somewhere else, it would travel about [feet] feet before the answer came back. The weed's behind it by then. That's why a decision tied to a moving machine has to be made on the machine.
>
> The slow jobs are different. A yield map can upload overnight from the shop. Next year's seed choice can wait a week.
>
> So before you buy a tool that needs a signal, do this. Map your own ground with your own phone. Then ask the dealer what the tool does in the spot where you got the worst reading. Ask for it in writing.
>
> That's what this phone read, on this carrier, on this afternoon. Yours will be different. That's the point.
>
> Phone only. Never from a moving machine.
>
