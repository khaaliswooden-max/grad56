# Field Test 12: The year's Field Test log (every reading, and what held up)

Module 12, *The Three Clocks*. Khaalis films; about 5 to 6 minutes finished. Horizontal 1920×1080 for the module page and YouTube; a vertical 1080×1920 cut of shots 4–6 and 9 for Shorts and Facebook.

**When to film:** this one can't be finished until the other eleven tests have been run. Modules 5 and 7 are filmed in season (spring and the summer peak of 2027), so film this test after the Module 7 test, in late summer or fall 2027. It opens with Module 12 on Dec. 7, 2027. The shot list and script below are complete now; the brackets wait for the readings.

What it shows: a measurement is only worth as much as the record kept of it. Eleven readings from one household, laid side by side, with the meter named, the day named, and an honest column for the ones that didn't match the module. It is the brand promise, on camera: we tell you when we were wrong.

## Safety and rules (read before filming)

- **Plug-in and handheld meters only.** Nothing is opened in this video or in any clip reused from the other eleven. If a reused clip shows anything that looks like an opened outlet, panel, appliance, or utility equipment, cut it.
- The on-screen line **"Plug-in meters only."** appears in shot 1 and on the closing card.
- Every reading is shown with its meter, its device, and its day, exactly as logged at the time. No reading is rounded toward the module's example, re-taken to look better, or left out.
- Phone-app readings (the sound meter in Module 5, the speed tests in Modules 9 and 10) are labeled **PLAUSIBLE**. Public-data figures (Modules 7 and 11) carry their source and data date. One household is never presented as a national figure.
- Where a reading didn't match what the module said, say so on camera and say what you think happened. If it exposes an error in a module, log it at getruralamericadone.com/corrections within 48 hours.

## Narration script

Read by Khaalis in his own voice, or rendered in Kokoro am_michael at speed 1.0 (then the video also carries "Narration: AI voice"). Brackets are filled in from the log; read numbers as words.

> This is a Grid and Grain Field Test. Plug-in meters only.
>
> For a year, we've said that anybody can check this for themselves. Today I'm checking us.
>
> This is every meter we used. About a hundred dollars' worth, all off the shelf. And this is the notebook.
>
> January. The outlet lost [volts] volts when the microwave started, on a [meter], on [date]. February. One load of dishes cost [peak] at the peak rate and [off-peak] off peak. March. The laptop drew [idle] watts idle and [busy] watts working. April. The dew point on the porch was [degrees]. May. The condenser read [a] at one meter and [b] at eight, on a phone app. June. The laptop wrote [words] words per joule. July. Pausing the window unit for the peak hour saved [watts] watts and cost [degrees] degrees. August. Four checks to find the dead battery out of [n]. September. [ms] milliseconds at the far fence. October. Three Wi-Fi points, and no internet when the one at the modem went dark. November. The sun's share was [x] percent at one o'clock and [y] percent at eight.
>
> Now the honest part. [Number] of those matched what the module told you to expect. This one didn't. The module said [what it said]. I measured [what I measured]. Here's what I think happened: [reason]. [If it exposed an error: We've corrected the module, and the correction is posted.]
>
> Three of these came from phone apps, so they're plausible, not verified. And all of it is one house, in one year. It's not a national figure, and we never said it was.
>
> Here's what the year adds up to. Demand for computing moves at chip speed. The power to serve it moves at grid speed. And your county decides on its own calendar. The gap between the first two is where you bargain.
>
> You've seen our readings. Write down yours.
>
> The whole Field Guide is free at getruralamericadone.com. Know the wires. Set the terms.
