# Field Test 06: A small language model on an everyday laptop (words per joule)

Module 6, *The Rural Hospital Runs on Compute*. Khaalis films; about 5 minutes finished. Horizontal 1920×1080 for the module page and YouTube; a vertical 1080×1920 cut of shots 5–8 for Shorts and Facebook. Can be filmed indoors in any season.

## Safety and rules (read before filming)

- **Plug-in meters only.** The meter plugs into the wall; the laptop's own charger plugs into the meter. Nothing is opened: not the laptop, not the charger, not the outlet. Not on camera, not off.
- The on-screen line **"Plug-in meters only."** appears in shot 1 and stays as a lower-third through every shot where the meter is in frame.
- Name the meter, the laptop, the model, and the day on screen and in the narration. Report the readings exactly as measured. One laptop, one model, one day: never present it as a national figure, and never set it beside a data center's number as a contest.
- Use an invented prompt. Never type or show real patient, family, or business information on camera.
- Charge the laptop to 100% and leave it plugged in for 10 minutes before the first reading. A charging battery adds watts that have nothing to do with the model.
- No hospital is named or shown. This test is filmed at home.

## The prompt (use exactly this, three times)

> Write about 300 words explaining to a new neighbor how a rural volunteer fire department is organized and paid for.

It is harmless, it has nothing to do with medicine, and it is long enough to time.

## Narration script

Read by Khaalis in his own voice, or rendered in Kokoro am_michael at speed 1.0 (then the video also carries "Narration: AI voice"). Brackets are filled in from the log; read numbers as words.

> This is a Grid and Grain Field Test. Plug-in meters only.
>
> It's [day of week], [month day], at my kitchen table in [town]. On the table: a [meter make and model], a plug-in watt meter, and an ordinary laptop, a [laptop make and model] from [year] with [memory] gigabytes of memory.
>
> The charger plugs into the meter, and the meter plugs into the wall. Nothing gets opened. The battery is full, so the meter is reading what the laptop is using right now.
>
> I've turned the Wi-Fi off. Everything you're about to see happens inside this laptop.
>
> Sitting idle, with the screen on, it draws [idle watts] watts.
>
> Now I'll ask a small language model, [model name], running in a free app called [app name], to write about three hundred words on how a volunteer fire department works. Go.
>
> Watch the meter. It climbs to [watts running] watts and stays there while the words come.
>
> Done. [seconds] seconds, [words] words.
>
> Here's the arithmetic. Watts times seconds is joules, the unit of energy. [watts running] times [seconds] is [joules] joules. Divide the words by the joules: [words per joule] words per joule.
>
> And notice the idle number. [idle watts] of those watts were there before the model did anything. A computer that's switched on costs energy whether it's working or waiting.
>
> I ran it three times. The readings are on screen. They're what this meter read, on this laptop, with this model, on this day. Yours will be different. That's the point: try it.
>
> Here's why it matters. Most of the A I tools a rural hospital is offered don't run in the hospital. They run in a data hall far away, and they need a good connection to get there. A small model in the building is slower and knows less, but the data never leaves, and it works when the internet doesn't. Which jobs belong where is the question to ask before anyone signs.
>
> Plug-in meters only. Never open a laptop or a charger.
>
> The whole module is free at getruralamericadone.com. Know the wires. Set the terms.
