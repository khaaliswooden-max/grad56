# Field Test 10: Unplug one mesh Wi-Fi point, then the one at the modem

Module 10, *Making the Deal*. Khaalis films; about 4 to 5 minutes finished. Horizontal 1920×1080 for the module page and YouTube; a vertical 1080×1920 cut of shots 5–9 for Shorts and Facebook. Can be filmed in December 2026; nothing about it is seasonal.

What it shows: copies are not independence. Three Wi-Fi points look like three backups. Unplug a far one and the house stays online. Unplug the one wired to the modem and every point is still lit, still broadcasting, and none of them reaches the internet. Three points, one path out.

## Safety and rules (read before filming)

- **Unplug at the wall only.** Each mesh point's power adapter comes out of its outlet and goes back in. Nothing is opened: no router, no modem, no outlet, no wall plate. Not on camera, not off.
- Don't unplug the modem itself, and don't touch the coax or fiber line coming into the house. The test is the mesh point *wired to* the modem, unplugged from power.
- Warn the household first. Internet drops for about two minutes in step 3. Don't run this during anyone's telehealth visit, work call, or while a medical or security device depends on the Wi-Fi.
- The on-screen line **"Plug-in meters only."** appears in shot 1, as on every Field Test. Add **"Unplug at the wall. Never open equipment."** as a lower-third through shots 5–9.
- Name the mesh system (make and model), the speed-test app, the internet provider's plan speed, and the day, on screen and in the narration. Report the readings exactly as measured. Phone speed-test readings are **PLAUSIBLE**, never VERIFIED. One house, one day: never present it as a general figure.

## Narration script

Read by Khaalis in his own voice, or rendered in Kokoro am_michael at speed 1.0 (then the video also carries "Narration: AI voice"). Brackets are filled in from the log; read numbers as words.

> This is a Grid and Grain Field Test. Plug-in meters only, and today, nothing gets opened. We're only going to unplug things at the wall.
>
> It's [day of week], [month day], at my house in [town]. I've got a [mesh make and model] mesh Wi-Fi system with [three] points. One of them is wired to the modem. The other [two] sit around the house and pass the signal along.
>
> Three points sounds like three backups. Let's find out.
>
> First, everything on. I'm standing in the [room] with cellular data turned off, running [app name]. I get [down] megabits down and [up] up.
>
> Now I unplug the far point, the one in the [room]. Wait a minute. Run it again. [down] down, [up] up. Slower, maybe, but I'm still online. The spare did its job.
>
> I plug that one back in and wait for its light. Now the other test. I unplug the point that's wired to the modem.
>
> Look at the other [two]. Still lit. My phone still shows a Wi-Fi signal. And the speed test won't run. There's no internet anywhere in this house.
>
> So I didn't have three backups. I had three copies hanging off one path. When that one path went down, they all went down together. Engineers call that a common-mode failure.
>
> Here's the arithmetic. Take two parts that each work ninety-nine percent of the time. If both have to work, you get about ninety-eight percent, which is around a hundred and seventy-four hours down in a year. Put them side by side, so either one will do, and you get ninety-nine point nine nine percent, under an hour a year. But only if they fail independently. If one thing can take out both, you're back where you started.
>
> Now read a deal the same way. A developer promises a park, a school payment, and a noise limit, and all three are owed by one company set up for one project. Three promises, one path. Independence looks like this: a bank holds the money, the obligations are recorded on the land, and somebody the company doesn't pay does the measuring.
>
> These readings are what one phone app showed, in one house, on one day. That makes them plausible, not verified. Your house will be different. Try it, and warn your family first.
>
> Plug-in meters only. Never open equipment.
>
> The whole module is free at getruralamericadone.com. Know the wires. Set the terms.
