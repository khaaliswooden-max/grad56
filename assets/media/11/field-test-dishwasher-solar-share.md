# Field Test 11: One dishwasher cycle, priced by the grid's hourly solar share

Module 11, *Small Is Possible*. Khaalis films; about 4 to 5 minutes finished. Horizontal 1920×1080 for the module page and YouTube; a vertical 1080×1920 cut of shots 4–8 for Shorts and Facebook. Can be filmed in December 2026; the data is published every day. A sunny day's data makes the clearest picture, so pick one from the past week.

What it shows: some jobs don't care what time they run, and the hour decides which power plants stand behind them. The dishwasher uses the same energy at 1 p.m. and at 8 p.m.; solar's share of the grid does not stay the same. Computers have jobs like the dishes (backups, scanning records, software updates), which is why a small shared computing site can be scheduled around the grid.

## Safety and rules (read before filming)

- **Public data only.** This test uses a government website and the yellow EnergyGuide label. Nothing is plugged in, unplugged, or opened. Most dishwashers are wired or plugged in behind the cabinet: do not pull the dishwasher out, and do not reach behind it.
- The on-screen line **"Plug-in meters only."** appears in shot 1, as on every Field Test, with **"Public data. Nothing to open."** as a lower-third through the screen-recording shots.
- Name the data source, the region (balancing authority), the date of the data, and the dishwasher's make and model, on screen and in the narration. Report the figures exactly as published.
- A regional solar share is not the mix at one house. Grade the result **PLAUSIBLE**, and say so. Never present one region and one day as a national figure.
- The grid monitor's data is near real time and can be revised later; the EIA says so itself. Screenshot the page with the date showing.

## Meter list

| Item | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| U.S. Energy Information Administration, Hourly Electric Grid Monitor | https://www.eia.gov/electricity/gridmonitor/ . Free, no account. Hourly generation by energy source is usually posted by 11 a.m. Eastern the next day, so use yesterday or earlier. |
| Your balancing authority | The grid operator for your area (for example, the regional operator your co-op buys through). If you don't know it, ask your utility or use the monitor's map. Name it on screen. |
| The dishwasher's yellow EnergyGuide label, or its model number | The label gives estimated kWh per year. Divide by 215 cycles to get kWh per cycle. If the label is gone, look the model up on the ENERGY STAR product finder, or use the ENERGY STAR ceiling for a standard model: 240 kWh a year, about 1.1 kWh a cycle (criteria effective July 19, 2023). |
| A calculator and a notepad | |
| Phone or camera on a tripod, plus a screen recording of the monitor | 1080p or 4K, 30 fps. |

## Narration script

Read by Khaalis in his own voice, or rendered in Kokoro am_michael at speed 1.0 (then the video also carries "Narration: AI voice"). Brackets are filled in from the log; read numbers as words.

> This is a Grid and Grain Field Test. Plug-in meters only, and today there's nothing to plug in. This one runs on public data.
>
> It's [day of week], [month day], in my kitchen in [town]. The dishwasher is a [make and model]. Its yellow label says [label] kilowatt-hours a year. That figure assumes two hundred fifteen loads, so one load is about [x] kilowatt-hours.
>
> Here's the question. Does it matter when I press start?
>
> This is the Hourly Electric Grid Monitor from the U.S. Energy Information Administration. It's free, and it shows what was making electricity in each region, hour by hour. I'm looking at [region] on [data date].
>
> At one in the afternoon, solar made [a] megawatt-hours out of [b]. That's [share] percent.
>
> At eight that evening, solar made [c] out of [d]. That's [share] percent.
>
> So one load at one o'clock leaned on the sun for about [y] kilowatt-hours. The same load at eight leaned on it for [z]. Same dishes. Same energy. Different power plants.
>
> Now, that's the share for the whole region, not for the wire into my house, so I'd call the result plausible, not verified. And it's one day. The agency says its hourly numbers can be revised.
>
> Here's why a county should care. Computers have jobs like the dishes. Backing up records, scanning old files, updating software: none of it has to happen at five in the afternoon. A small shared computing site can run that work when the grid has room, and stay out of the way when it doesn't. The nine-one-one call can't wait. The backup can.
>
> Try it with your own region and your own label. Public data only. Nothing to open.
>
> The whole module is free at getruralamericadone.com. Know the wires. Set the terms.
