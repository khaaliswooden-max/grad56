# Field Test 02: One dishwasher cycle, priced at peak and off-peak

Module 2, *Who Pays for the Substation?* Khaalis films; about 4 to 5 minutes finished. Horizontal 1920×1080 for the module page and YouTube; a vertical 1080×1920 cut of shots 5–9 for Shorts and Facebook. Can be filmed in any month.

## Safety and rules (read before filming)

- **Plug-in meters only.** The meter plugs into the outlet; the appliance plugs into the meter. Nothing is opened: no appliance panel, no outlet cover, no junction box. Not on camera, not off.
- **Use the dishwasher only if it has a cord and plug you can reach without tools**, usually in the cabinet under the sink. Opening a cabinet door is fine. If the dishwasher is hardwired, or the plug is behind the machine, **don't pull the machine out. Use the washing machine instead** (a 120-volt washer on a wall outlet) and say so on camera. The lesson is identical: one cycle's kilowatt-hours, priced twice.
- The meter must be rated for 15 A. Check the appliance's rating label first; if it lists more than 12 A, pick the other appliance. Never use an extension cord or a power strip between the outlet, the meter, and the appliance. Keep the meter off the floor of the sink cabinet, away from any drip.
- Never put the meter on a 240-volt appliance (an electric dryer or range). Those plugs won't fit it; don't adapt them.
- The on-screen line **"Plug-in meters only."** appears in shot 1 and stays as a lower-third through every shot where the meter is in frame.
- Name the meter, the appliance, the cycle, the co-op or utility, the rate schedule, and the day. Report readings exactly as measured. One appliance, one cycle, one rate sheet: never present it as a national figure.

## Narration script

Read by Khaalis in his own voice, or rendered in Kokoro am_michael at speed 1.0 (then the video also carries "Narration: AI voice"). Brackets are filled in from the log; read numbers as words.

> This is a Grid and Grain Field Test. Plug-in meters only.
>
> It's [day of week], [month day], in my kitchen in [town]. This is a [meter make and model], a plug-in watt meter rated for fifteen amps. And this is my dishwasher, a [make and model]. Its cord plugs into an outlet under the sink, so the meter goes between the two. Nothing gets opened. If your dishwasher is wired in with no plug, don't pull it out. Use the washing machine.
>
> Meter reset to zero. Normal cycle. Start.
>
> While it fills, it barely sips: [watts] watts. When the heater comes on, it jumps to [watts] watts. That's the part that costs.
>
> [Cycle length] later, the meter reads [kilowatt-hours] kilowatt-hours for the whole cycle.
>
> Now the rate sheet. I'm on [co-op or utility], on the [rate schedule] rate. From [on-peak hours], a kilowatt-hour costs [on-peak cents] cents. The rest of the day it costs [off-peak cents] cents.
>
> So the same load of dishes costs [on-peak cost] cents if I run it at suppertime, and [off-peak cost] cents if I run it at bedtime. Same dishes. Same machine. The only thing that changed was the hour.
>
> That's one meter, one cycle, and one rate sheet, on one day. Your numbers will be different. Try it.
>
> Here's why it matters past the kitchen. Power costs more at the hours everyone wants it, because the whole system is built for that peak. Now picture a customer that reserves as much capacity as a whole county. The co-op builds a substation for it. If that customer uses less than it reserved, or leaves, somebody still pays for what was built. A minimum bill says the customer pays for what it reserved, used or not. It's one of six terms in a large-load tariff, and as of May twenty twenty-six, twenty-three states had approved at least one. That's the Edison Electric Institute's count, reported by Columbia's Sabin Center, and it's verified.
>
> The checklist is free at getruralamericadone.com. Plug-in meters only. Know the wires. Set the terms.
